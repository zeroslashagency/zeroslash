### Vision

Redesign the "What we do / Premium services that grow your brand" section as a **layered, spherical composition** inspired by Netflix's "Our Living World" nested-spheres poster. Each concentric sphere represents a different service category that ZeroSlash offers, with its own macro floral photograph and text overlays creating a "nature-meets-niche taxonomy" visual philosophy.

<Callout id="design-references" tone="info">

**Reference Images:**
- **Image #1**: Netflix "Our Living World" — nested spheres with outer layer (leopards/"DISCOVER"), middle layer (sharks/"THE SECRET"), inner core (lizard/insects/"THAT CONNECTS US ALL")
- **Image #2**: "Gulaabi Sunday" chrysanthemum — dense purple flowers with scattered text labels ("POST", "STORY", "TYPO", "CHRYSANTHEMUM", etc.) overlaid directly on floral imagery

</Callout>

### Design Direction

Create a fun, experimental, nature-inspired visual where each service category is represented by:
- A **distinct macro floral photograph** (different flower species per layer)
- **Text overlays** positioned on/within the flower imagery
- **Nested circular composition** showing hierarchy (outer → inner spheres)
- **Depth and layering** that reveals the full taxonomy on interaction

Three decisions are now settled and reflected throughout: the photography is **stock** (Unsplash / Pexels), the layers carry a **subtle scroll parallax**, and mobile uses a **simplified two-layer nest** rather than falling back to cards. Full detail in Decisions Locked at the end.

### Floral Photography Strategy

Each layer uses a **distinct flower species** shot in macro/close-up style, matching the botanical aesthetic from prior "approach swatches" work:

<Table
  id="floral-mapping"
  columns={["Layer", "Flower Species", "Color Palette", "Visual Characteristics"]}
  rows={[
    ["Outer: Web Development", "Dahlia or Sunflower", "Warm oranges, yellows, golds", "Bold, architectural petals; repeating geometric patterns"],
    ["Middle: Marketing", "Rose or Peony", "Deep pinks, magentas, reds", "Layered, lush petals; organic chaos meets structure"],
    ["Inner: Branding", "Chrysanthemum (ref: Image #2)", "Purples, violets, deep burgundy", "Dense, intricate petal structures; complex texture"],
    ["Core: Product Design", "Anemone or Ranunculus", "Deep blues, teals, blacks", "Delicate center detail; intimate scale; focused core"]
  ]}
/>

<Callout id="photo-sourcing" tone="decision">

**Decided: stock photography (Unsplash / Pexels).** No custom shoot, no AI generation. Both licenses allow commercial use without attribution, so the four hero images can ship straight into `public/images/services/`.

Selection rules that keep the four spheres reading as one family:
- Dark or near-black background so white overlay text stays legible and the circular mask has no visible seam
- Single flower filling the frame, roughly centered — the crop is a circle, so anything important near the edges gets cut
- Square or near-square source (crop to 1:1 before export); portrait shots lose the petal edges
- Shallow depth of field, natural light. Skip anything with visible studio gradients, watermarks, or heavy Instagram-style color filters
- Pull all four from a similar exposure range; if one is much brighter, correct it rather than swapping in a mismatched shot

Search terms per layer: `macro dahlia dark background`, `peony macro dark`, `chrysanthemum macro purple`, `anemone flower dark macro`.

</Callout>
